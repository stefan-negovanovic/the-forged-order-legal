# Privacy Policy — The Forged Order

Last updated: 11 October 2026

The Forged Order is a strength and boxing training app made by Stefan Negovanović, an
individual developer ("I", "me"). This policy explains what the app does with information about
you. The short version: your training stays on your phone, there is no account, and the app sends
only technical data, to report crashes, to measure how often it starts without crashing, and to
check for updates. It never sends your name, your email or your training data.

## What stays on your phone

Everything you enter stays on your device and is never sent to me. That includes your answers to
the onboarding questions, your weekly plan, your chosen warrior, your training sessions, sets,
weights and repetitions, your XP and level, and your settings. The app has no account, no
sign-in and no sync of its own.

**Android backup.** Like most Android apps, the app allows Android's own backup. If backup is
turned on for your phone, Android may save a copy of the app's data to your Google account, under
Google's terms, and restore it when you set up a new phone or reinstall the app. I have no access
to that backup. You can turn it off in your phone's settings (usually Settings → System →
Backup), and you can delete it from your Google account.

## What the app sends, and why

The app sends information off your phone in three cases.

**1. Crash and error reports, to Sentry.** When the app crashes or hits an unexpected error, it
sends a report to [Sentry](https://sentry.io), a crash-reporting service. I use these reports
only to find and fix bugs. A report contains:

- the error and its stack trace (which lines of the app's code were running);
- a trail of recent events before the error ("breadcrumbs"): the names and times of steps the
  app performed (for example, that a set was logged), random identifiers the app generated, and
  system events such as the app being opened or sent to the background, the screen turning on or
  off, taps on the app's controls, and changes in connectivity, battery or power saving. They do
  not contain the values you entered;
- technical details of the device and the app: the manufacturer and model, the operating system
  and its version, whether the device is rooted or an emulator, the language and time zone
  settings, battery level and charging state, memory and storage use, screen size and
  orientation, network type, the app's version, and the identifier, channel and runtime version
  of the app update it is running;
- an anonymous, random installation identifier created by Sentry, which lets me tell whether
  several reports come from one installation. It is not linked to your name, email, phone number
  or any other account.

Reports contain no name, no email address, no contacts, no location and none of your training
data. The app is built never to log what you enter, and it strips stored values from database
errors before a report leaves your phone.

**2. App sessions, to Sentry.** Each time the app is opened, and when you return to it after a
while, it tells Sentry that a session started, how long it lasted, and whether it ended in a
crash or an error, with the app's version and the same anonymous installation identifier. I use
this only to measure how often the app runs without crashing.

Sentry stores reports and sessions in the European Union and does not keep your IP address with
them. They are deleted automatically within 90 days. Crash reports and sessions are sent only by
the app builds that are distributed for testing and in the store, not by development builds.

**3. Update checks, to Expo.** When it starts, the app asks Expo's update service (EAS Update)
whether a newer version of its code is available, and downloads it if so. The request contains
the platform (Android), the app's runtime version and update channel, the identifiers of the
update it is running and of updates that recently failed to start, a random installation
identifier, and, if the app failed to start after an update, the technical error that stopped
it, which can include the app's internal identifiers for your installation. It contains no name,
no email and none of your training data. Like any web request, it reaches Expo's servers from
your IP address. Expo's handling of this data is described in
[Expo's privacy policy](https://expo.dev/privacy).

## What the app does not do

- No advertising and no advertising identifiers.
- No analytics or tracking beyond the crash reports and sessions above.
- No selling or sharing of data with anyone for any purpose other than the services above.
- No access to your location, contacts, camera, microphone or files.

## Notifications

The app can remind you when a rest period between sets ends. These reminders are scheduled on
your phone and shown by your phone. No notification passes through a server, and you can turn
them off at any time in your phone's settings.

## The app store

If you install the app from Google Play, Google collects information under its own privacy
policy, independent of the app. This policy covers only the app itself.

## Deleting your data

Your training data is deleted when you clear the app's data in your phone's settings, or when you
uninstall it. If Android backup is on, also delete the app's backup from your Google account, or
reinstalling the app may restore it. I cannot recover your data, because I never have a copy.

Crash reports and sessions are deleted automatically within 90 days. Because they are anonymous, I
usually cannot tell which ones came from your phone. If you want those from your installation
deleted sooner, write to me and I will delete every one I can link to it.

## Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to ask what data about you I hold, to have it
corrected or deleted, and to object to its use. Because the app keeps your data on your phone
and sends nothing that identifies you, in most cases there is nothing about you that I can find.
You can still write to me at any time, and you can complain to the data protection authority
where you live.

## Children

The app is not directed at children under 16, and I do not knowingly collect information from
them.
